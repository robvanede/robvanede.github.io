Goodbye Danube

  • By Rob in Bicycle touring, Travel blog

Today I have left the Danube after having followed this river for 2350 kilometers. The weather decided to make it a sad day, as it has been raining until well after noon.

The start was early today, as I was told that the ferry to the Bulgarian side would only leave at 9 A.M. and 6 P.M.. After I had arrived, after 5 kilometers of cobblestone riding, the border official told me the ferry would be leaving at 10.30 today. Great: 2 hours of waiting in the rain. Thankfully they had a shelter and I was even invited to join the border officials for coffee.

When the ship finally arrived at the other side (after a 10-minute crossing), all I could really do was to go to a tea-room and have some chays with the locals of Nikopol. Around 2, the rain finally slowed down and I could get moving. My final destination for today became a small pension in the town of Levski, in rural Bulgaria.

Romanian roads

  • By Rob in Bicycle touring, Travel blog

Horse and cart are a very normal way of moving people and their belongings from place to place. Some carts are filled with wood or furniture, others carry whole families, as we normally do in our cars. I have also seen many ‘parked’ horses at the side of the road in the towns, probably waiting for their owners to return from their grocery shopping a in mini-market.

Today, I must have left behind a 175 kilometer long string of cheering, waving and high-fiving people. This is how it must feel to be a celebrity. Obviously, most people here have never seen a bike like mine, but they are so openly enthusiastic about it. Really great to make people’s day by just pedaling through their villages. The 175 kilometers was possible because of a raging storm that was building up behind me, creating an almost gale-force tail-wind. For the second half of the day, my speed has rarely fallen below 30 kilometers per hour, with peaks over 40 and that on flat terrain! I was in Turnu Magurele just an hour or two before the storm and am now very happy to be in a warm (although a bit expensive) hotel with wind and rain outside the window. Tomorrow and the coming weeks should be fine again

One other interesting thing: today I noticed that a clock at the side of the road was an hour ahead of my cycle computers time. After checking, it turned out that I had crossed the time-zone border when I entered Bulgaria yesterday. Lucky that I found it out today, as I’ll have to catch the 9 A.M. ferry to Nikopol, a ferry that only sails twice a day…

  • Romania

33 kilometers through endless Bulgarian hills

  • By Rob in Bicycle touring, Travel blog

While talking with the Bulgarian border officials (they obviously had very little to do on the small border crossing near Bregovo), they suggested that riding on the highway to Vidin was a better option than the route along small roads that I had in my book. Traffic would be no problem and there was only one hill. The best of it all: 33 instead of 62 kilometers.

They were right about most things, but the hill was pretty big. I did get to Vidin in about 1.5 hours, which was pretty good. While riding on this highway, I hardly saw any car. What a waste it has been to build this road…

After arriving in Vidin early, I had all the time for not finding a hotel, so I decided to move on the the other side in the end: Romania.

The Iron Gate

  • By Rob in Bicycle touring, Travel blog

Today I rode through the part that is called the Iron Gate, supposedly the most spectacular part on the Danube. From what I have seen so far, this is indeed the best part so far. The river has cut itself through the rocks here, which resulted in a gorge that is only 150 meters wide at it’s most narrow part, with a water depth of 80 meters!

The downside of all this natural beauty is that the terrain was not completely flat. Not at all really, with climbing percentages up to 10%. This was the first time I encountered these climbs, but it actually went pretty well. That certainly boosts the confidence for Bulgaria next week!

After a 140 km trip (accommodation was very scarce in this area), I have now arrived in Kladovo, a nice little town in Serbia, with ugly Romanian industry within eyesight at the other side of the Danube.

  • Iron Gate, Kladovo

Ferry from Stara Palanka to Ram

  • By Rob in Bicycle touring, Travel blog

This is the sort of ferry service you don’t see in Western Europe: It was just a barge with a loosely attached boat on the side to power it. Also, there was no real ramp, just a dirt road ending at the riverside. The barge was just ‘crashed’ into the dirt, after which some men with hand-shovels tried to close the gap so far that cars could actually drive on and off the thing. It also left half-an-hour late, as the crew were drinking beers and sleeping at a table from a restaurant next to the ‘ramp’. We (two German cyclists and me) made it to our destination just before dawn.

Sep 14

-5 star accommodation

  • By Rob in Bicycle touring, Travel blog

But the food was good!

It happened like this:

After about 110 kilometers of riding through rainy weather (of which 7 kilometers involved pushing the bike over a muddy path that had become unrideable because of the rain), I decided to look for some accommodation in the town of Karavukovo. I stopped at a bar and asked. The lady asked if I could speak German and started to phone someone, then pushed the phone in my hands. It turned out that her husband has a small old restaurant 8 kilometers out of town. He gave me directions and I went there. There was not much luxury and I did not dare to drink the water because of a very metallic smell that you could taste when only washing your face. That must also be the source of the rusty traces in everything that has something to do with water. For dinner, there was nothing to choose: it was two types of fresh fish, roasted over wood-fire, with some bread and fresh tomatoes and paprika. Just delicious. This more than made up for the ambiance of being the only guest with a host than could only speak a tiny bit of German. He went home to the village to leave me to sleep at his restaurant after the meal and came back to serve me a breakfast of bread and two hot sausages.
